Study Notes: Linear Equations, Graphs, and Systems

Section 1: Linear Equations in One Variable
A linear equation in one variable is an equation that can be written in the form ax + b = 0, where a and b are real numbers and a is not equal to zero.
To solve a linear equation, we isolate the variable using inverse operations:
1. Apply the distributive property to eliminate parentheses.
2. Combine like terms on each side of the equation.
3. Use the addition or subtraction property of equality to move variable terms to one side and constant terms to the opposite side.
4. Use the multiplication or division property of equality to isolate the variable coefficient to 1.
An identity is an equation that is true for all real numbers (e.g., 2x + 4 = 2(x + 2)). A contradiction is an equation with no solution (e.g., x + 1 = x + 5).

Section 2: Slope and Graphing Linear Equations
The Cartesian coordinate system uses two perpendicular axes (the horizontal x-axis and the vertical y-axis) intersecting at the origin (0,0).
The slope of a line represents its rate of change and steepness. Given two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2), slope m is calculated as m = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1).
- A positive slope rises from left to right.
- A negative slope falls from left to right.
- A horizontal line has a slope of zero (y = c).
- A vertical line has an undefined slope (x = c).

Forms of Linear Equations:
1. Slope-Intercept Form: y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is the y-intercept (0, b).
2. Point-Slope Form: y - y1 = m(x - x1), where m is the slope and (x1, y1) is a point on the line.
3. Standard Form: Ax + By = C, where A, B, and C are integers and A >= 0.

Section 3: Systems of Linear Equations
A system of linear equations consists of two or more linear equations involving the same set of variables.
The solution to a system of two linear equations in two variables is the ordered pair (x, y) that satisfies both equations simultaneously. Graphically, the solution corresponds to the point of intersection of the lines.
There are three possible outcomes:
1. One unique solution: The lines intersect at exactly one point (independent system).
2. No solution: The lines are parallel with equal slopes and different y-intercepts (inconsistent system).
3. Infinitely many solutions: The equations represent the exact same coincident line (dependent system).

Solving by the Substitution Method:
1. Solve one of the equations for one variable in terms of the other variable.
2. Substitute the expression into the second equation to produce a single-variable equation.
3. Solve the resulting equation.
4. Back-substitute the value into one of the original equations to solve for the remaining variable.

Solving by the Elimination (Addition) Method:
1. Multiply one or both equations by non-zero constants so that coefficients of one variable are opposites.
2. Add the two equations together to eliminate that variable.
3. Solve for the remaining variable.
4. Substitute the value back to find the second variable.
